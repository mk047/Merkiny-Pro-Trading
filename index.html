How this backtest strategy works

1. Trend filter (MA20) — A 20-period simple moving average defines bias. Price above MA → only long setups are considered. Price below MA → only short setups.

2. Pullback / rejection entry
• Long: price is above MA20, the bar’s low tags or dips near the MA, and the candle closes bullish with a longer lower wick (buyers defended the pullback).
• Short: price is below MA20, the high rejects near the MA, and the candle closes bearish with a longer upper wick.

3. Risk & targets — Stop distance = max(1.2 × ATR(14), 0.4% of price). Position size = (account × risk%) ÷ stop distance. Take-profit is fixed at 2R (twice the stop distance). If neither TP nor SL hits within 30 bars, the trade exits at market (time stop).

4. Metrics — Win rate, net P/L, max drawdown, profit factor (gross wins ÷ gross losses), and average R are computed from the simulated trades on the loaded history. Starting paper equity is $10,000.

1. What is Trading & How Markets Work Beginner ▼

Trading is the act of buying and selling financial instruments (stocks, forex, crypto, commodities, indices, futures) with the goal of profiting from price changes. Unlike investing (long-term ownership), trading focuses on shorter timeframes and active risk management.

Key Market Types

  • Stocks / Equities — ownership shares of companies (AAPL, TSLA, NVDA).
  • Forex (FX) — currency pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD). Open 24h on weekdays.
  • Crypto — digital assets (BTC, ETH). Nearly 24/7 markets.
  • Commodities — gold, silver, oil, natural gas, agricultural products.
  • Indices — baskets of stocks (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, DAX).
  • Futures / Perpetuals — leveraged contracts (like Binance Futures). You trade notional exposure with margin; profit/loss is marked to market.

Bid, Ask & Spread

Every instrument has a bid (price buyers are willing to pay) and an ask (price sellers want). The difference is the spread — your immediate cost of entering a trade. Tighter spreads = better liquidity.

Long vs Short

  • Long (Buy) — you profit if price rises.
  • Short (Sell) — you profit if price falls. You “borrow” and sell, then buy back cheaper later.

Spot vs Futures (why this app feels like Binance Futures)

  • Spot — you own the asset; max loss is what you paid.
  • Futures / Perps — you post margin and control a larger notional. Leverage amplifies both gains and losses. Liquidation can close the position if margin is exhausted.
On the Paper Futures page practice Long/Short with leverage, TP/SL and isolated margin — the same workflow professionals use on Binance Futures and TradingView charts.
2. Risk Management — The Foundation of Survival Beginner ▼

Most new traders lose money because they risk too much per trade. Professional risk management is non-negotiable.

The 1% Rule (or less)

Never risk more than 0.5–1% of your total account equity on a single idea. On a $10,000 account that means a maximum loss of $50–$100 per trade.

Position Sizing Formula

Position Size = Risk Amount ($) ÷ Distance to Stop (in $ per unit)

Example: Account $10,000, risk 1% = $100. You buy a stock at $50 with a stop at $48 (risk $2 per share). Size = 100 ÷ 2 = 50 shares.

Reward-to-Risk Ratio

Aim for at least 1.5R to 2R. If you risk $100, your target should be $150–$200 or more. This lets you be profitable even with a win rate below 50%.

Stop-Loss & Take-Profit

  • Always define your invalidation level (stop) before entering.
  • Place stops beyond structure (beyond swing high/low or key level), not arbitrarily.
  • Avoid moving stops further away once in a trade (that increases risk).
After 2–3 consecutive losses, step away. Emotional revenge trading destroys accounts faster than any bad strategy.

Candlestick Anatomy

  • Wicks (shadows) — high and low of the period. Long wicks show rejection of price.

4. Support, Resistance & Market Structure Intermediate ▼

Price tends to react at previous swing highs (resistance) and swing lows (support). These levels form the backbone of technical analysis.

Identifying Levels

  • Look for areas where price reversed multiple times.
  • Round numbers and previous day/week highs/lows often act as magnets.
  • Once broken, support can become resistance and vice-versa (role reversal).

Market Structure

  • Uptrend — higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL).
  • Downtrend — lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
  • Range / Consolidation — price oscillating between clear horizontal boundaries.

A break of structure (e.g., taking out a previous swing low in an uptrend) is an early warning that the trend may be changing.

Draw horizontal lines at clear swing points on the chart. Practice waiting for price to return to those levels before entering.
5. Trend Following, Moving Averages & Momentum Intermediate ▼

Moving Averages (MA)

Simple Moving Average (SMA) or Exponential (EMA) smooth price. Common periods: 20, 50, 200.

  • Price above rising 50/200 MA → bullish bias.
  • Golden Cross (50 crosses above 200) / Death Cross (opposite) are classic signals but lag.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Oscillator 0–100. Above 70 often called overbought; below 30 oversold. In strong trends RSI can stay elevated/depressed for long periods — use it more for divergence than pure overbought/oversold.

MACD

Shows relationship between two EMAs. Histogram expansion = momentum building. Crossovers and divergences can signal shifts.

The Dashboard chart already loads RSI and a simple MA. Use them as context, not as standalone buy/sell signals.
6. Volume, Volatility & ATR Intermediate ▼

Volume confirms price moves. A breakout on high volume is more trustworthy than one on low volume.

ATR (Average True Range)

Measures average volatility over a lookback (usually 14). Use ATR to:

  • Set stop distance (e.g., 1.5 × ATR beyond the level).
  • Size positions so that volatility does not force you out of good trades.
  • Avoid trading when ATR is extremely compressed (breakout likely) or extremely expanded (choppy).
High-volatility periods (news, earnings, open of major sessions) require smaller size or wider stops.
7. Trading Psychology & Process Advanced ▼

The best strategy in the world fails without discipline. Psychology is the edge that separates consistent traders from gamblers.

Common Biases

  • FOMO — entering late because price is “running away”.
  • Revenge trading — increasing size after a loss to “get it back”.
  • Confirmation bias — only seeing evidence that supports your existing view.
  • Recency bias — overweighting the last few trades.

Process Over Outcome

Judge yourself on whether you followed your plan (entry criteria, risk, stop, target), not on whether the trade won or lost. A good process with a losing trade is still a win for your development.

Journaling

Record: setup type, reason for entry, risk in $, emotional state, outcome, lesson. Review weekly. The Growth page has a journal for this purpose.

Build a pre-trade checklist and force yourself to answer every item before clicking Buy or Sell.
8. Building a Simple, Robust Strategy Advanced ▼

A complete strategy needs clear rules for: market selection, bias, entry, stop, target, and risk.

Example Framework (Trend-Pullback)

  1. Higher timeframe (Daily/4H) in clear uptrend (HH + HL, price above 50 MA).
  2. Wait for a pullback into prior support, MA, or Fibonacci zone on lower timeframe.
  3. Enter on bullish rejection candle or break of minor structure.
  4. Stop below the pullback low (or 1.5× ATR).
  5. Target previous high or measured move (at least 1.5–2R).
  6. Risk ≤ 1% of equity.

Backtesting & Forward Testing

Before risking real (or even paper) capital seriously, review historical charts and mark every occurrence of your setup. Then paper-trade it live for at least 20–30 trades while journaling.

Start with one setup on one or two markets. Mastery of a simple edge beats a complex system you cannot execute consistently.

Major Sessions (UTC approximate)

  • Asia — 00:00–09:00 (Tokyo, Sydney)
  • London — 07:00–16:00 (highest FX volume)
  • New York — 12:00–21:00 (overlap with London is most liquid)

High-Impact News

CPI, NFP, FOMC, central-bank speeches, earnings. Spreads widen and slippage increases. Many traders stay flat or reduce size 15–30 minutes around the release.

Use the News page and external calendars (ForexFactory, Investing.com, TradingView Economic Calendar) to stay aware.

Never hold a large position into an unknown high-impact event unless that is an explicit part of your tested plan.

11. Futures Mechanics — Leverage, Margin & Liquidation Advanced ▼

This module mirrors how Binance Futures and similar platforms work. MERKINY PRO paper trading uses the same concepts so you build real muscle memory.

Notional vs Margin

Notional = Size × Entry Price
Initial Margin ≈ Notional ÷ Leverage

Example: Long 0.5 BTC at $60,000 with 10× leverage → Notional $30,000 → Margin required ≈ $3,000.

Isolated vs Cross

  • Isolated — only the margin assigned to that position can be lost. Safer for learning.
  • Cross — entire account balance backs all positions. Higher capital efficiency, higher risk of cascading liquidation.

Liquidation (simplified)

When unrealized loss approaches the margin posted, the position is force-closed. Rough isolated long liq estimate:

Liq ≈ Entry × (1 − 1/Leverage) (ignoring fees & maintenance margin for education)

High leverage shrinks the distance to liquidation. On the order ticket, watch the “Est. liq.” line before you click Long/Short.

Take-Profit & Stop-Loss

Always define both. On MERKINY PRO you can attach TP/SL at entry so the paper engine knows your invalidation and target — the same discipline required on live futures.

Practice sizing so that a full stop-out risks ≤ 1% of equity even when leveraged. Leverage is a position-sizing tool, not a “get rich” button.
